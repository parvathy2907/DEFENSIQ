Signature impact quantification estimates the relative impact of behavioral signatures observed during malware analysis. In this project, it provides severity-related scores for the attack behaviors identified from Cuckoo Sandbox reports.

The project uses the **MAGIC** impact-quantification approach [15], developed by the project's guide and co-guide (Rohini S, Gowtham Ramesh and Anand R Nair). It combines initial severity-based score assignment with a refinement stage based on signature co-occurrence and mutual information. This allows the initial score of a signature to be evaluated in the context of other behaviors observed in the malware repository. The refined score is the impact score A<sub>k</sub> used by the residual-severity and CCEA stages.

The resulting impact scores are used by the subsequent attack–defense evaluation and CCEA-based optimization stages.

:::note[Functional Separation]
Signature impact quantification and residual-severity estimation perform distinct functions:
- **Signature Impact Quantification:** Quantifies the standalone attack impact associated with an observed behavioral signature across the repository.
- **Residual-Severity Estimation:** Evaluates the severity remaining when a specific candidate defensive mechanism is applied against that signature.
:::

---

## Purpose of Impact Quantification

Malware samples may trigger multiple behavioral signatures during execution. These signatures can represent different activities and may occur together as part of a broader attack sequence. Assigning an impact score to each signature provides a structured way to represent the relative significance of the observed behaviors.

However, an initial severity value alone may not represent the context in which a behavior occurs. The co-occurrence of signatures can provide additional information about how behavioral patterns are associated within the collected ransomware repository.

The project's impact-quantification stage addresses this by refining the initial signature scores using repository-level co-occurrence information and mutual-information values. The resulting scores provide a consistent numerical representation of behavioral impact for downstream analysis.

The purpose of this stage is to support the comparison of attack signatures. It does not independently determine whether a particular defensive mechanism mitigates a behavior; that assessment is performed by the subsequent residual-severity evaluation component.

---

## Initial Severity Assignment

The first pass of the impact-quantification algorithm assigns each signature an initial score based on its malware-analysis severity value, denoted as *V<sub>x</sub>* (Cuckoo's own severity for the signature), and on how frequently the signature occurs in the repository.

The algorithm uses four severity bands:

| Band | Severity Category | Description |
|:---:|:---|:---|
| <span class="band b1">B1</span> | Low | Minor operational telemetry, non-damaging diagnostic actions, or low-risk probing. |
| <span class="band b2">B2</span> | Medium | Evasive behavior, persistence establishment, or defense modification attempts. |
| <span class="band b3">B3</span> | High | Unpacking, process hollowing, privilege escalation, or security control disabling. |
| <span class="band b4">B4</span> | Critical | Mass encryption, volume shadow copy deletion, or active ransomware extortion mechanisms. |

Each signature is initially placed into a band according to its malware-analysis severity score and its frequency. Within the selected band, its position is determined relative to signatures that have already been placed in that band.

For the ransomware implementation, the project uses a score range of **[1, 35]**. This range was selected based on the central tendency and statistical dispersion of the behaviors represented in the repository.

:::note[Algorithmic Boundary Rule]
The numerical boundaries between bands and the ranking procedure within a band follow the MAGIC algorithm [15]. The band labels are descriptive; they are not the boundaries themselves.
:::

The output of this pass is the initial impact score assigned to each signature.

---

## Severity Bands

Severity bands provide a categorical organization of signatures according to their initial malware-analysis severity. They divide the configured score space into low, medium, high, and critical categories.

The project uses four bands, whose boundaries come from the score-assignment procedure. They are stages of the initial scoring process, not a universal cybersecurity severity standard.

The use of bands helps organize signatures with different severity values and supports relative ranking within a band. The subsequent refinement stage considers signature co-occurrence information to determine whether the initial score should be updated.

:::tip[Experimental Consistency]
The score range and its band boundaries should remain consistent with the configuration used for the experiment. Any changes to the range or band-assignment procedure must be documented so that the resulting scores remain reproducible.
:::

---

## Co-Occurrence Analysis

Co-occurrence analysis examines how often behavioral signatures appear together across the malware samples represented in the repository.

A sample may trigger several signatures during execution. Examining these combinations provides information about the relationships between observed behaviors across the collected dataset.

The project's impact-quantification method uses **Signature Co-occurrence Count (SCC)** to quantify co-occurrence information across the knowledge base. For a particular signature, the method considers its co-occurrence with the other signatures triggered by the sample.

The co-occurrence stage performs the following operations:

1. **Identify triggered signatures:** Extract the signatures triggered by each analyzed sample.
2. **Examine behavioral combinations:** Analyze combinations of signatures observed executing together.
3. **Aggregate co-occurrence frequency:** Quantify joint frequencies using Signature Co-occurrence Count (SCC).
4. **Cross-reference knowledge base:** Align behavioral combinations against the existing ransomware knowledge base.
5. **Supply refinement parameters:** Feed aggregated co-occurrence values to the mutual-information refinement stage.

:::caution[Correlation vs. Causation]
Co-occurrence indicates that signatures appear together in the analyzed data; it does not by itself establish that one behavior causes another. The information is used to refine signature impact scores rather than to infer unsupported causal relationships.
:::

## Mutual-Information Refinement

The second pass refines the initial signature scores using mutual-information-based information derived from signature co-occurrence.

For a signature *S<sub>x</sub>*, the algorithm examines its relationship with the remaining signatures triggered by the relevant sample. Mutual-information values are aggregated across signature pairs to calculate a weight denoted as *W<sub>x</sub>*.

The resulting weight is compared with a dynamic threshold:

- **If *W<sub>x</sub>* exceeds the threshold:** The signature score is updated according to the implemented refinement procedure.
- **Otherwise:** The existing score is retained.

This process allows the algorithm to consider information from related behavioral patterns rather than relying exclusively on the initial severity value.

### Refinement Procedure

The method follows seven structured steps:

1. Retrieve the initial impact scores assigned during the first pass.
2. Identify the relevant signature co-occurrence information from the repository.
3. Calculate the mutual-information values used by the implementation.
4. Aggregate the relevant values to obtain *W<sub>x</sub>* for each signature.
5. Compare *W<sub>x</sub>* with the configured dynamic threshold.
6. Apply the implemented score-update procedure when the threshold condition is met; otherwise, retain the existing score.
7. Return the refined signature impact scores.

:::note[Formula details]
The mutual-information formula, aggregation procedure, dynamic-threshold calculation and score-update equation follow the MAGIC formulation [15].
:::

## Algorithm Inputs and Outputs

The algorithm receives the signature information and repository-level evidence required for initial scoring and refinement.

### Inputs

- **Behavioral signatures:** The signatures extracted from the Cuckoo Sandbox analysis reports.
- **Initial severity values (*V<sub>x</sub>*):** Malware-analysis severity scores used during initial placement.
- **Execution records:** Information identifying which signatures were triggered by each analyzed sample.
- **Signature Co-occurrence Count (SCC):** Repository-level information describing the co-occurrence of signatures.
- **Ransomware knowledge base:** Existing behavioral information used by the refinement method.
- **Scoring configuration:** The configured score range [1, 35], severity bands (B1–B4), and threshold-related parameters.

### Outputs

The algorithm returns a refined impact score for each processed signature. These values retain the association with their original signature identifiers so that the results can be used by subsequent components.

The scores are then available for use in the attack–defense evaluation and the CCEA fitness-calculation stage.

### Formal Algorithmic Procedure

```text
Algorithm: Mutual-Information-Based Signature Impact Refinement
Input:   Triggered signatures, initial severity values (Vx), execution records,
         repository-level co-occurrence information (SCC), scoring configuration [1, 35].
Output:  Refined severity-related impact scores for processed signatures.

Procedure:
  1. Assign initial scores to signatures using malware-analysis severity values (Vx).
  2. Place signatures into configured severity bands (B1: Low, B2: Medium, B3: High, B4: Critical).
  3. Rank signatures relative to existing signatures within each band.
  4. Retrieve co-occurrence information (SCC) for signatures observed together in analyzed samples.
  5. Calculate mutual-information values across signature pairs.
  6. Aggregate mutual-information values to compute weight Wx for each signature.
  7. Compare each weight Wx with the dynamic threshold.
  8. If Wx > Dynamic Threshold:
       Update score using the implemented refinement rule.
     Else:
       Retain existing initial score.
  9. Return refined impact scores.
```

## Limitations

The impact-quantification method depends on the quality and coverage of the collected malware repository:

1. **Repository Dependency:** Signatures that rarely occur in the sample population provide limited co-occurrence evidence, potentially leaving their initial severity bands unrefined.
2. **Dynamic Attack Evolution:** Behavioral patterns observed in the current repository may not capture unseen variations or future ransomware evasion tactics.
3. **Correlation vs. Causation:** Co-occurrence indicates empirical correlation across runtime traces; it does not establish deterministic causal dependencies between attack stages.
4. **Scope Limitation:** A refined impact score represents the standalone behavioral significance of an attack signature; it does not measure the severity remaining after a defense is applied.

:::note[Evaluation Separation]
In this project, refined impact scores are combined with separately estimated residual-severity values during defense-combination evaluation. This clear separation allows the impact-quantification stage and the LLM-based residual-severity stage to be assessed independently according to their respective objectives.
:::

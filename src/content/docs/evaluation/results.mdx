## Residual-Severity Estimation (LLM Mode)

The local MiST-8B model assessed the 144 signature–defense pairs of Sample02 (25 signatures, 24 distinct D3FEND defense names), with one inference call per resolvable pair.

- **Defense resolution:** 20 of the 24 defense names resolved in D3FEND. Four (Mandatory Access Control, Data Backup, Registry Access Control and System Policy Restrictions) returned `not_found_in_d3fend`, so 15 pairs were skipped and 129 were assessed. Six signatures had no resolvable candidate at all.
- **Validity:** every assessed row carries a numeric RS no higher than its initial severity.
- **Outcome** (label derived from RS relative to the initial severity: FULL if RS = 0, NONE if RS equals the initial severity, otherwise PARTIAL): 2 FULL, 5 PARTIAL, 122 NONE. Only 5 of the 25 signatures (creates_exe, exe_appdata, creates_shortcut, moves_self and uses_windows_utilities) have any candidate that reduces severity.
- **Benchmark metrics:** QWK, normalized MAE, exact and ±1 accuracy and macro-F1 require independently labelled reference RS values, which have not yet been produced. Reference labelling is in progress; these metrics will be reported in the final submission.

CCEA found its best combination at generation 12 and stopped at generation 32, with RA = 0.561 and F* = 0.060: the recommended defenses remove only 6.0% of the severity, and 94% remains. So 6.0% is the best combination found with the default budget, not the best possible: with a larger budget (G<sub>max</sub> = 2000, τ = 500), CCEA selects Executable Denylisting for creates_shortcut at generation 248 and reaches F* = 0.068 (RA = 0.556), the highest value any combination of these assessed candidates can reach. In CSV mode, the same sample gives F* = 0.810. The two RS sources therefore lead to very different conclusions for the same sample.

## Discussion of Results

- **The pipeline works end to end.** On every sample, the recommended combination removes most of the measured severity (mean MSS 0.638 → 0.134). The co-evolutionary credit mechanism also searches significantly better than chance under the same evaluation budget.
- **Budget sensitivity.** The coverage-plus-patience rule stops after about 58 generations in spaces of up to 10³⁷ combinations. A larger budget raises the mean F* from 0.790 to 0.812 (G<sub>max</sub> = 500) and 0.826 (G<sub>max</sub> = 2000), so the default configuration trades a small amount of fitness for a much shorter run.
- **The RS source matters most.** In the Sample02 study, the provisional CSV table implies that the recommended defenses remove 81% of the severity, while the schema-valid (not yet benchmarked) MiST-8B assessments leave 94% in place. Optimization quality is bounded by RS quality, which is exactly research gap G4.
- **Extending the objective.** The fitness can be extended to judge RS on the whole combination, to credit one defense covering several signatures, and to include cost and overlap; the co-evolutionary structure can be extended to handle these.

## Attack Evolution Results

**Protocol.** Each of the 17 samples is evolved once with the parameters of the defense CCEA (p<sub>e</sub> = 0.25, p<sub>m</sub> = 0.20, τ = 20, G<sub>max</sub> = 100), with RS from the CSV table. All 17 samples were evolved in under a minute. The method is on the [Attack Evolution](/methodology/attack-evolution/) page.

**The defense still works on evolved malware.** The mean defense score is 0.789 on the original samples and 0.744 on the evolved samples (mean drop 0.045), so the defense still removes about 74% of the attack severity. For 16 of the 17 samples the attack found a harder sample. For Sample12 it found none and the sample stays unchanged. The attack stopped after a median of 43 generations (28 to 100); one sample reached the limit of 100. Of the 225 signatures that were swapped in, 145 (64%) were related signatures and 80 came from the random choices.

**The best defense follows the attack.** Of the signatures that stay in the evolved sample, 113 keep the same best defense and 115 get a different one; the 225 swapped-in signatures each receive a new defense. For Sample01, 19 of 26 signatures were swapped (12 related, 7 random), the defense score moved from 0.794 to 0.759, and the search stopped after 32 generations, with the hardest sample found in generation 12.

**Found in the public repository.** For 12 of the 17 evolved samples, the evolved signature list contains the complete signature list of at least one real ransomware sample in MLRan that uses a swapped-in signature (between 5 and 79 real samples, from 2 to 25 families). For the other five samples (Sample04, 10, 11, 12 and 16) no such real sample exists.

**How to read this.** The defense search is randomised, so part of the change in F comes from the search and not from the attack. We therefore do not use the drop as a measure of how strong the evolved attack is. The result we claim is that the defense generator re-optimises for every evolved sample and still removes about 74% of its severity. Swapped-in signatures borrow rows from another sample, and the repository check shows that the behaviors occur together in real ransomware, not that the evolved malware would run.
